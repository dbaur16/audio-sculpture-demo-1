About this simulation — methodology + sources Show ↓

What this is
This is a rhetorical simulation, not a technical replication of any specific classifier. It is designed to make visible the documented bias logic of mainstream audio ML systems — not to claim it runs CLAP, MERT, or any other model.

What the classifier simulation models
The cold inner layer penalizes high spectral flux (rapid unpredictable change), high entropy (energy spread across many frequencies), and moments outside a narrow mid-range frequency band. It rewards periodicity — repeating patterns that sit stably on a regular grid. These penalties are grounded in documented failure modes of pretrained audio models on non-Western music.

Empirical grounding
CLAP-Music scores 80.1% ROC-AUC on Western pop corpora and 61.5% on Carnatic music — an 18.6 point drop that grows monotonically with cultural distance from Western pretraining data. Source: Papaioannou, Benetos & Potamianos, "Universal Music Representations?" ISMIR 2025 (preprint).

94% of total training hours in the field's standard corpora are Western music. African music across an entire decade of conference output totals 28 hours. Source: Mehta et al., "Music for All," arXiv 2025 / NAACL 2025.

The three-stage framework
This project is situated within a broader analysis of how musical misrecognition moves from editorial choice (a person decontextualizes) → platform coercion (an algorithm imposes a schema) → training data (flattening inscribed before any model trains). By stage three, nobody is making a choice. There is simply no field for Raag Megh. Source: Hodgson, "Unruly Musics," Unruly Intelligences in Music AI, UCLA DataX Pilot 2026.

What this does not model
Tempo and beat grid detection bias; key/scale detection forcing microtonal music into 12-TET; timbre classification trained on Western instrument taxonomies; training data corpus skew at the embedding level. A full technical replication would require running actual model embeddings — which is the research direction of the Unruly Intelligences project.
